# Skin Lesion Lab: a model comparison that needed a second cohort

This is a portfolio experiment in binary melanoma classification from dermoscopy images. It is not a diagnostic tool. I compared an HSV-feature logistic regression baseline with a fully fine-tuned EfficientNet-B0, then applied their saved models and decision rules to a separately collected image cohort. The interesting result is the gap between a clear development comparison and a much less certain external one.

The local development export matches the [ISIC 2018 Challenge Task 3 training collection](https://api.isic-archive.com/collections/66/) by its recorded fields and counts, although the original download receipt is unavailable. The [challenge data terms](https://challenge.isic-archive.com/data/) and local metadata specify attribution and noncommercial use under CC-BY-NC. No source images or row-level metadata are redistributed here. The development report covers 1,395 selected images: 176 melanoma and 1,219 benign. Its 1,037 groups join known lesions and duplicates, but patient identifiers were unavailable. I fixed the label and split rules before comparing models, kept calibration and cost cutoffs away from the held-out images, and ran the prespecified seeds 17, 42 and 73. The full matrix has 27 completed runs, including small CNN and transfer-learning variants. The primary comparison, however, was full unweighted EfficientNet fine-tuning against HSV logistic regression.

Mean image-level ROC-AUC was 0.891 for that EfficientNet model and 0.755 for logistic regression. The paired difference was 0.1354, with a 95% interval from 0.0950 to 0.1761. That interval resamples known lesion/duplicate components within class and conditions on three fitted seeds. It does not account for unidentified shared patients or a new site's case mix. The sensitivity difference was -0.0076, with an interval from -0.0720 to 0.0552. Better ranking on development images did not establish better sensitivity under the saved decision rules.

Calibration and referral make this distinction concrete. The primary model's mean Brier score fell from 0.0790 before calibration to 0.0755 afterward, but calibration did not help every comparator. At the locked cutoffs, the three EfficientNet runs missed 18, 26 and 11 of 176 melanoma images before referral. The saved referral rules retained between 47.4% and 75.7% of development images. Retained-set error rates describe a different set of images; referring a case does not make its prediction correct. The 10:1 error cost used to choose a threshold is an illustrative experiment setting, not a clinical utility estimate.

I then tested the same primary pair on the original HIBA release, Hospital Italiano de Buenos Aires Skin Lesions Images (2019-2022). The frozen protocol retained 884 contact-polarized dermoscopy images: 194 melanoma and 690 selected benign images in 370 linked components. It excluded clinical photos and diagnoses outside the original task. The image-overlap audit found no exact cross-source matches; independent inspection cleared 160 perceptual candidate pairs. Cohort-local IDs cannot prove that no person appears in both sources. The release is [CC-BY with institutional attribution](https://api.isic-archive.com/doi/hospital-italiano-de-buenos-aires-skin-lesions-images-2019-2022/). No patient photograph appears in this article or the slides.

All six frozen external runs completed without refitting, recalibration or threshold selection. EfficientNet's mean ROC-AUC was 0.7966 and logistic regression's was 0.7485. The prespecified paired difference was 0.0481, with a 95% component-bootstrap interval from -0.0016 to 0.1091. It includes zero, so this cohort does not clearly establish an EfficientNet advantage on the primary endpoint. The external component intervals use 2,000 shared, unstratified draws; they condition on the three fitted seeds. Deep-model ROC-AUC ranged from 0.7653 to 0.8438 across those seeds.

Under the frozen cutoffs, mean sensitivity was 0.4863 for EfficientNet and 0.4536 for logistic regression. EfficientNet missed 75, 124 and 100 of the 194 melanoma images across seeds before referral. Its saved referral rules retained 40.16%, 80.77% and 63.01% of images, with 12, 87 and 46 false negatives still in the retained sets. These are poor grounds for a safety claim. The cohort is retrospective, mostly light skin types, and differs from the development cohort in prevalence and acquisition. I have not established performance by skin tone, patient-level generalization across sources, or clinical usefulness.

The project is a reproducible implementation and an exercise in honest evaluation. The [notebook sources](../notebooks/) load the accepted development report and show the run-linked comparison, calibration and error counts. The [benchmark protocol](benchmark.md) and [external evaluation contract](external-evaluation.md) explain how the results were made. The independent external audit recomputed all nine reported endpoints and intervals from frozen predictions. The work does not establish a new method or support a preprint on its current evidence. Its strongest lesson is narrower: a development ranking can look decisive while a separately sourced test leaves the primary comparison unsettled.
